Backup and Recovery

Intermediate 🇫🇷 Français

Why Back Up Your Data?

Imagine all the files in your office disappearing overnight — client contracts, financial reports, ongoing projects. A backup is your safety net: a copy of your data stored separately, ready to be restored when something goes wrong.

Real-world analogy: Backups work like photocopies of your important documents kept in a safe at the bank. If a fire destroys your office, you can rebuild your files from the copies stored in a secure location.

Incidents that can cause data loss are more common than you might think:

  • Human error — accidentally deleting an important file
  • Hardware failure — a hard drive or server breaking down
  • Cyberattack — ransomware encrypting your data
  • Natural disaster — flooding or fire affecting your premises

Essential Terms

  • Full backup — a complete copy of all your data. Like photocopying every single file in your cabinets.
  • Incremental backup — a copy of only the data that has changed since the last backup. Like photocopying only the new pages that were added.
  • Snapshot — a point-in-time image of a volume's state. On OPCP, Ceph RBD snapshots are instantaneous using Copy-on-Write technology.
  • Restore point — a specific moment in time you can return to. Like a bookmark in time that marks the state of your data at a given moment.
  • RPO (Recovery Point Objective) — the maximum amount of data you can accept losing, measured in time. An RPO of 1 hour means you could lose at most 1 hour of work.
  • RTO (Recovery Time Objective) — the maximum acceptable time to restore your data after an incident. An RTO of 4 hours means your systems must be operational within 4 hours.

The Backup Process

The backup process in CloudStore follows several automated steps. Once configured, it runs without manual intervention.

What happens at each step

  • Scheduling — You define when backups should occur (e.g., every night at 2 AM).
  • Selection — The system identifies which data has changed since the last backup.
  • Transfer — Data is encrypted then sent to CloudStore via a secure channel.
  • Storage — The backup is stored on-premise on your OPCP rack.
  • Verification — CloudStore verifies the copy is intact and sends you a confirmation.

The Recovery Process

Recovery (or restoration) is the reverse process: retrieving your data from a backup when an incident occurs. It's like going to the bank's safe to collect your photocopies after a disaster.

What happens at each step

  • Detection — An incident is identified (deleted file, corrupted data, system failure).
  • Point selection — You choose which point in time you want to return to (e.g., "yesterday at 6 PM").
  • Extraction — CloudStore retrieves the corresponding backup from its secure local storage.
  • Restoration — Data is placed back into your working environment.
  • Validation — You verify that the restored data is complete and functional.

Ceph RBD Snapshots and Recovery

In addition to traditional backup workflows, OPCP's Ceph RBD block storage provides powerful snapshot and recovery capabilities built directly into the storage layer:

Instant Snapshots (Copy-on-Write)

Ceph RBD snapshots use Copy-on-Write (CoW) technology, which means:

  • Zero initial copy time — snapshot creation is instantaneous, regardless of volume size. A 16 TB volume snapshots just as fast as a 1 GB volume.
  • Space-efficient — only blocks that change after the snapshot consume additional space.
  • No performance impact — taking a snapshot doesn't interrupt or slow down the running workload.

Using Snapshots as Restore Points

Each snapshot represents the exact state of a volume at a specific moment. You can:

  • Roll back a volume to any previous snapshot state
  • Maintain multiple snapshots for different points in time
  • Create pre-update snapshots before risky changes (database migrations, OS updates)

CoW Cloning

Copy-on-Write cloning creates new volumes from existing snapshots instantly:

  • The clone is immediately usable — no waiting for data copy
  • The new volume shares unchanged data with the parent snapshot
  • Only modified blocks consume additional space
  • Ideal for creating test environments from production data, or rapidly provisioning identical systems

RBD Mirroring for Disaster Recovery

RBD mirroring provides asynchronous replication between Ceph clusters:

  • Replicate volumes between OPCP sites for geo-redundancy
  • Asynchronous replication minimises impact on primary workload performance
  • In case of a site-level disaster, failover to the replicated cluster
  • Supports both one-way and bidirectional mirroring configurations

Official documentation: For detailed information about Ceph RBD snapshot and mirroring capabilities on OPCP, refer to the Ceph RBD overview guide.

Backup Best Practices

The 3-2-1 rule: Keep 3 copies of your data, on 2 different types of media, with 1 copy offsite. On OPCP, combine CloudStore backups with Ceph RBD snapshots and RBD mirroring to achieve comprehensive protection.

Combining strategies on OPCP

  • Ceph RBD snapshots for fast, frequent restore points — ideal for short-term recovery (hours to days).
  • CloudStore (S3) backups for longer-term data archival and compliance retention.
  • RBD mirroring for cross-site disaster recovery when multiple OPCP installations exist.

Tips for customer conversations

  • Test regularly — A backup that has never been tested is not reliable. Recommend quarterly restoration tests.
  • Match frequency to change rate — The more often data changes, the more frequent backups should be.
  • Use RBD snapshots before risky operations — Always snapshot before database migrations, OS updates, or major configuration changes.
  • Set priorities — Not all data is equally important. Critical data deserves more frequent backups and snapshots.
  • Document the plan — In an emergency, everyone needs to know who does what and in what order.

Key Takeaways

  • Backup creates safety copies of your data to protect against loss.
  • Recovery restores your data from those copies when an incident occurs.
  • The backup process is automated: scheduling, selection, transfer, storage, and verification.
  • The recovery process is guided: detection, point selection, extraction, restoration, and validation.
  • Ceph RBD snapshots provide instant, zero-copy-time restore points using Copy-on-Write technology.
  • CoW cloning creates new volumes from snapshots instantly for testing or rapid provisioning.
  • RBD mirroring enables cross-site disaster recovery through async replication between Ceph clusters.
  • Combine RBD snapshots, CloudStore backups, and RBD mirroring for a comprehensive protection strategy.
